- 2026-08-19 — **One card model across the ecosystem: the hub's duplicate rate engine is DELETED and card semantics now resolve from PickMe's catalogue via `CreditCard.contractCardId`.** Full design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-card-identity-collapse-design.md`. (1) **What was found:** the hub carried TWO complete, non-overlapping card engines for the same 27 cards — `src/lib/cards/presets.ts` (1,458 hand-authored lines) → `CreditCard.rewards` → `CardRewards`, powering `/cards`, `/cards/[id]`, `/cards/manage`, the card form, `fees.ts`, `/api/cron/notify` and `/api/events`; versus `contracts/card-catalogue.json` → `src/engine/cards-twin`, powering `/bills`, wallet-events, cap-usage and ownerState. **They shared zero consumers.** Decision 2 fired against `src/engine/cards/` and never covered this larger duplicate because it sat in a different directory under a different name. (2) **The duplicate was the weaker model**, and this is why the collapse direction was forced rather than chosen: the catalogue expresses MCC include/exclude, country/currency/channel predicates, `accountYear` caps with anchor and reset timezone, `postCapEarn`, FX free-allowance with post-allowance rate, effective-dated `announced` rules and proration; `CardRewards` projected all of it onto a fixed 11-category enum. Provenance decided it too: **121 of 121 catalogue rules are `issuerConfirmed` with a source URL and `lastVerifiedAt`**, while a preset's provenance was a `highlights: [...]` array of marketing strings. (3) **The ids had drifted and nobody could see it:** 27 presets, 27 catalogue cards, the same cards by exact `officialName`, but **only 10 of 27 ids agreed** (`td-aeroplan-vi` vs `td-aeroplan-visa-infinite`, `crypto-indigo` vs `cryptocom-royal-indigo`, +15 more). Annual fee and network agreed on all 27, which is what made the 1:1 mapping safe to trust. (4) **D3's "No open card editor (quality moat)" was being violated in this repo**: `src/components/card-form.tsx` was 1,460 lines letting any user author `categoryRates`, `capGroups`, `conditions`, `merchantRates` and `baseRateOverrides` for any card. **Owner ratified removing it in session, against the advice to sequence it separately.** The add-card flow is now: pick from the catalogue, then fill in facts about your own copy. An uncatalogued card routes to the new `/cards/request` page — D3's demand-driven expansion path, which had a `CardRequest` model and an API but **no UI in the hub at all** until now; removing hand-authoring without it would have left those users with no route. (5) **The boundary that replaced it:** the catalogue says what the CARD is (earn rules, caps, FX, credits, benefits); `CreditCard` says what YOUR COPY is (nickname, lastFour, limit, statement/due day, APR, fee dates); `OwnerState.cardStates` holds your ANSWERS and syncs both ways; `CardState`/`CapUsageLedger` hold your ACTIVITY. Nothing rate-shaped is stored per user any more. (6) **`CreditCard.rewards` is dropped; `feeRebateMinor` replaces its one genuinely per-user field.** The old model flattened a fee waiver to a single number — Scotia Gold as `$120.00` — where the catalogue records what the issuer actually says ("Ultimate Package can rebate up to $150; Preferred can rebate $40"). A tiered offer cannot be one number, so the **owner** states their own rebate and the catalogue's prose is shown as guidance; no figure is ever inferred. Migration `20260819200000_card_semantics_from_catalogue` backfills it from the enabled `rewards.conditions` so nobody loses a rebate already recorded. (7) **The catalogue gained `credits`** (Swift + schema + zod mirror + TS twin), 7 across 5 cards, because it had no concept of statement credits and `benefits-catalogue.json` is insurance only (verified: 4 families, 10 kinds, no credits, no waivers). Safety property that made this cheap: credits are **not** earn rules, `RecommendationEngine` never reads them, so the 27 golden fixtures were provably unaffected — and were confirmed unaffected by running them. (8) **Honesty cost, recorded not hidden:** those 7 credits migrated from unsourced hub presets and are labelled **`sourceType: "inferred"` — the only non-issuer-confirmed rows in the whole catalogue.** They must be verified against the issuer and promoted to `issuerConfirmed` before launch, or D3's bar is no longer literally true. (9) **Retired as duplicates of things the catalogue already had:** `toggleCardCondition` and the whole `conditions` model (the catalogue already carries `ownerConditions` — `amazonEligiblePrimeLinked`, `cryptoLevelUpProActive`, `rogersEligibleServiceLinked`, `tangerineCategorySelected` — wired to `CardState`), and `addCapUsage` plus its manual `CardState.capsUsage` path (`CapUsageLedger`/`CapAccrual`, fed by wallet capture, is the real cap ledger). (10) **Verified, not assumed:** hub 84 files / 786 tests green and `tsc` clean; `next build` succeeds across 77 pages; PickMe Engine at 186 tests with the **same 7 pre-existing failures as before the change** (all assertions of one test, `AcquisitionAnalyzerTests.testAcquisitionUsesPortfolioDeltaNotCandidateGrossRewards`, already broken on `main` — untouched, still broken, and worth its own fix); Store package builds; catalogue re-validated against its JSON Schema. - 2026-08-19 — **`PUT /api/spine/owner-state` is a MERGE, not a replace, and `GET` now exists.** The endpoint used to `upsert` `stateData` wholesale, which is last-writer-wins with no conflict detection: correct for one writer, silently lossy for two. PickMe writes its whole wallet on every setup save (`CheckoutFlowView`), so a card set or condition answer authored on the web vanished the next time the phone saved. `reconcileOwnedCards` already protected `ownedCardIds` with an additive union; the object around it had no such protection. Ratified policy, now enforced in `mergeOwnerState`: **`ownedCardIds` union — never remove, because a writer that cannot see a card is not evidence the card was sold**; `cardStates` merged per CARD not per field, so a card the writer never mentioned keeps its stored answers while a card it did mention is replaced entirely (which is what makes un-ticking an answer still work); `switchThreshold`/`valuationsCad`/`carry`/`ownerStateVersion` last-writer-wins, because averaging two whole-object preferences produces a third setting neither client asked for; `defaultCardId` never left dangling. Concurrency reuses the optimistic `updateMany`-guarded-on-`updatedAt` pattern from `reconcileOwnedCards`, but with a bounded retry and a **409 rather than a false success** — that function converges on the next read because it is idempotent, whereas this one is carrying a user's edit. `GET` returns `ensureOwnerStateRecord`, with `ownerState: null` a real answer (no catalogue-linked cards yet), never an error. PickMe gained `fetchOwnerState()` and seeds its picker on first run **only when there is no local wallet**, so it can never overwrite one — closing the original complaint: the same account no longer re-enters its cards on each platform.

- 2026-08-18 — **E4's deferred design item is BUILT: MarketLens serves a dynamic per-user symbol set, and the hub consumes it end to end.** Full design in `marketdata/docs/decisions/0003-dynamic-quote-path.md`. (1) **Endpoint facts, probed live — do not re-derive:** Yahoo's `/v8/finance/chart` answers **200 with a plain User-Agent and no cookie/crumb**, so a pure-Java client works (no yfinance sidecar); its multi-symbol `/v7/finance/quote` answers **401**, so **there is no batch endpoint** and N symbols cost N requests — the quote path fans out with a semaphore, a deadline, and a per-request cap instead. A bad symbol answers 404, cleanly separable from throttling. (2) **`GET /api/v1/quotes?symbols=`** is cache-first from `price_candle`, measures staleness against the exchange calendar, and returns `FRESH` / `STALE` (with `staleTradingDays`) / `UNAVAILABLE` **with a null price** — never interpolated, never zero-filled. Demand-registered symbols live in `tracked_symbol`, deliberately separate from the curated `watchlist_symbol` (different lifecycle, provider, and quota); it holds **symbols only**, never consumer identity or quantities. A nightly sweep warms them so no page render depends on a live fetch. (3) **Routing is by purpose, not one global provider:** curated watchlist → Alpha Vantage (sanctioned, 25/day), dynamic per-user set → Yahoo (E4). **Both are daily closes** — the split is sanction and quota, *not* latency; Alpha Vantage's free tier is not real-time either, and that framing is barred by A6. Providers resolve through a new `MarketDataProviderRegistry` keyed by `(AssetClass, capability)`; the registry was **required, not optional** — `IngestionService` injected a single `MarketDataProvider` bean, so E4's second provider would have failed startup with `NoUniqueBeanDefinition`. `QuotaService` is now per-provider; it was hard-wired to `ALPHAVANTAGE`/25, so Yahoo would have silently drained the watchlist's budget. (4) **Correctness gap found and closed, not in the record:** `price_candle` had **no currency column**, and neither did `Holding`. Invisible with four US tickers; wrong the moment a user's symbol set arrives, since VFV.TO is CAD and AAPL is USD and a total that adds them is fabricated. Currency is now stored and returned, **nullable meaning "the provider did not say" — never "assume USD"**; the hub's `holdingsValuation()` excludes mismatched-currency holdings from an account total and returns them so the UI must disclose them. Provider-sourced prices are never written without a currency, so a null `Holding.priceCurrency` can only ever mean "typed in by hand", which is what makes reading it as the account currency safe. (5) **BYOK shipped (`X-Provider-Key: PROVIDER=key`)**: used for one request, never persisted, never logged; keys live encrypted in the hub via the existing `secretCrypto` envelopes, at `/settings/providers`. Chain is caller key → app key → keyless → cache → UNAVAILABLE, and every quote reports `keySource` so falling back to an unlicensed source is *stated*, never silent. This is also the honest exit from E4's accepted Yahoo risk: a user who brings a key is priced under their own licence. The `keys.html` provider key is now labelled an in-memory **session override** — it never survived a restart, which on a spin-down plan was a silent surprise; persisting it was rejected because MarketLens has no encryption at rest and must not become a credential holder. (6) **MarketLens is a standalone product, not a hub appendage** (owner's ruling): the quote path and BYOK ship as public capabilities with OpenAPI, a `quotes.html` dashboard, and demo-mode support. Standing boundary: **no portfolio-valuation endpoint** — symbols in, prices out; the hub does the multiplication and the FX. (7) **Four defects caught by testing, three of which no unit test could have found:** a not-found symbol tripped the circuit breaker (it counts every RuntimeException, so a few typo'd tickers would starve real ones); the parallel fan-out raced on quota find-or-create, and catching the duplicate-key error was *not* enough because a failed statement aborts the whole Postgres transaction (25P02) — fixed with a single conditional `UPDATE` plus `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`; `CHAR(3)` for currency would have passed every test and all of demo mode then failed **on deploy** under `ddl-auto: validate`, which compares it as `Types.CHAR` against the entity's VARCHAR; and demo mode **invented a confident price for any symbol string**, including nonexistent ones — the exact fabrication the product promises never to produce, in the surface a recruiter sees first. (8) **Verified, not assumed:** all nine Flyway migrations applied to a real Postgres 16 (the `ALTER` propagated to partition children and the table stayed partitioned), the app booted against it under `ddl-auto: validate`, live quotes returned mixed USD/CAD with a bogus symbol failing closed, and the hub's client → sync plan → valuation chain ran end to end against the live service. **Not verified locally:** `MarketdataApplicationTests` (Testcontainers) — it only probes `/var/run/docker.sock`, which needs root to symlink on this machine; it runs in CI. (9) **Crypto valuation is ratified as MarketLens' (identity change, mirrored to all four repos), build deferred to next session.** Planned: **Binance public data** for history (`data.binance.vision`, probed: 200, no key, *published for bulk download* — a materially better risk posture than Yahoo; it quotes **USDT, not USD**, which the new currency column now carries), CoinGecko for spot via one app-level key. The hub's CoinGecko path is explicitly **on loan**, not drift. **Questrade was evaluated and rejected for pricing:** per-user OAuth with a refresh token that rotates on every use (a human must re-issue it from the account UI after one missed rotation, fragile in a cron), it requires holding a Questrade account so it can never be a general provider, and its real value is **holdings sync** — the hub's domain, and gated on whether A1's "no bank credentials ever" covers a brokerage credential. **Open, needs a ruling before anyone builds it.**

- 2026-08-18 — **Naming research for E1 (hub consumer name) — findings recorded, name still OPEN.** Three waves, 20 names collision-checked, 50+ domain lookups. (1) **The 4–5 character domain space is exhausted on `.com` AND `.ca` alike** — 0 of 8 free using pronounceable *nonsense* strings, because any 4–5 letter string is a real word in some language. Real words are claimed at ANY length (`fidus`, `custos`, `fidelis`, `fiducia`, `custodia` all taken on `.ca`). `.ca` only diverges from `.com` at 6+ chars, where it is much freer (CIRA's Canadian Presence Requirement excludes global squatters). **Consequence: "1–2 syllables + exact registrable domain" is impossible. Pick two of {short, exact-match domain, available today}.** (2) **Dead — do not re-check:** Keel (UK BaaS, exited stealth 05/2026), Reckon (ASX-listed accounting, RKN), Trove (Nigerian investing app), Tally, Fathom (fathomhq financial reporting), Kestrel (agri neobank + Kestrl Islamic fintech), Alder (Alder Financial ×2, Aldermore), Lintel (UK banking), Croft (Croft Finance OS), Sheaf (sheaf.ca — tracks subscriptions/licences/renewals, uncannily close to us), Tenon (Tenon Financial aggregates accounts "into one view"), Quoin (Quoin Ventures portfolio app + Qoins), Tarn (usetarn.com = crypto portfolio tracker), Larder (three live food companies), Vellum (vellum.ai owns the search results). **Only survivor of 20 on class-36 headroom: Marlow** — owner didn't connect with it. Run the domain-variant sweep BEFORE the search. (4) **Tooling traps, cost me two wrong answers:** plain `whois` is unreliable for new gTLDs (falls through to IANA and returns a TLD description that reads as a hit — this is why `marlow.money` was first reported taken when it is free); `rdap.org` returning 404 is ambiguous, meaning EITHER available OR no-RDAP-server-for-this-TLD (it wrongly cleared `marlow.io`, registered since 2018). Use the authoritative per-TLD RDAP from `https://data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json` and always run a control (known-registered → 200, gibberish → 404) before trusting a batch. `.ca` = `https://rdap.ca.fury.ca/rdap/`, `.com` = `https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/`, Identity Digital (`.money`/`.finance`/`.cash`) = `https://rdap.identitydigital.services/rdap/`. (5) **Free as of today** (all control-verified): `muwwakil.com` + `muwwakil.ca` — the ONLY exact-match `.com` found in the whole search, available because it is 8 chars with a doubled-w; `marlow.money`/`.finance`/`.cash`; and on `.ca`: `wakala`, `wakalat`, `mandata`, `credere`, `servata`, `thiqa`/`thiqah`/`thika`, `amanya`, `amanata`.
